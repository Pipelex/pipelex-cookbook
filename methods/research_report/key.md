# Key: ev_battery_density

Inputs: the inline `question` "What are the most promising approaches to improving battery energy density for electric vehicles?", the question the example has always shipped with. The method drafts it from three angles out of the model's own knowledge, looks at each angle's main claim a second time, and renders its brief through a fixed Markdown template, so this key checks the template's structure and the substance a sound answer carries.

## Planted facts
F1. The question is about the energy density of electric-vehicle batteries, not about charging speed, price or charging infrastructure.
F2. The approaches the field treats as the most promising include solid-state electrolytes, silicon or silicon-carbon anodes, lithium-metal anodes, lithium-sulfur cells, high-nickel or other high-capacity cathodes, and cell-to-pack or structural pack designs that raise density at the pack level.
F3. Most of these approaches carry open problems: dendrites and interface stability for lithium metal and solid-state cells, silicon's swelling, the polysulfide shuttle and short cycle life of lithium-sulfur, and the cost of manufacturing at scale.
F4. The report template is fixed: a "Research Report" title, then the sections "Question", "Executive Summary", "Key Findings" and "Open Questions", closed by a horizontal rule and a footer line.

## Must
M1. The report's `text` opens with the heading "# Research Report" and holds, in this order, the sections "## Question", "## Executive Summary", "## Key Findings" and "## Open Questions".
M2. The "Question" section repeats the question word for word: "What are the most promising approaches to improving battery energy density for electric vehicles?"
M3. The "Executive Summary" section is a non-empty paragraph about raising the energy density of electric-vehicle batteries.
M4. The "Key Findings" section holds at least three bullet lines, each starting with "- ".
M5. The key findings name at least three of these approaches: solid-state electrolytes, silicon or silicon-carbon anodes, lithium-metal anodes, lithium-sulfur cells, high-nickel or other high-capacity cathodes, and cell-to-pack or structural pack designs.
M6. The "Open Questions" section holds at least one bullet line naming an unresolved issue, such as cycle life, dendrite formation, silicon swelling, safety, manufacturing cost or scale-up.
M7. The report ends with a horizontal rule followed by the line "Generated by Pipelex".

## Must not
N1. A template placeholder left unrendered, such as `$brief`, `$question`, `{{` or `{%`.
N2. A list printed as a Python literal, such as `['…', '…']`, instead of bullet lines.
N3. An empty section, or the word `None` standing in for a section's content.
N4. A report about another subject, such as charging infrastructure or battery recycling, that leaves out the approaches to energy density.
N5. A key finding stating that all-solid-state or lithium-sulfur cells are already in mass-produced electric vehicles.

## Also acceptable
A1. An approach named in other words, such as "solid electrolytes" for solid-state electrolytes, "Si anodes" for silicon anodes or "CTP" for cell-to-pack, for M5.
A2. Bullets of more than one sentence, for M4 and M6.
